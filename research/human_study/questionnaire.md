# Questionnaire and response coding

Status: **draft, not yet cognitively tested or validated.** Use the exact approved
wording and item order in a preregistered study. Randomize only item blocks marked
as randomizable.

## Eligibility and prior use

1. Are you at least the legal age of adult consent in your location? (yes / no)
2. Can you comfortably read and evaluate AI answers in English? (yes / no)
3. How often have you used a text-based AI assistant in the past three months?
   (never / once or twice / monthly / weekly / daily or almost daily)
4. Which systems, if any, have you used? (multi-select plus prefer not to say)

Do not collect demographic attributes unless a reviewed scientific need and an
appropriate privacy plan are added to the protocol.

## Domain familiarity

For each included domain: "How familiar are you with this subject?"

1. New to it
2. Some familiarity
3. Comfortable with common concepts
4. Experienced
5. Highly experienced
6. Prefer not to say

This is used to select contrasting domains and describe the sample, not to infer
identity or competence beyond the task.

## Consent comprehension

- Is participation voluntary? (yes / no / unsure)
- Should you enter confidential work or personal information? (yes / no / unsure)
- Can AI answers and inferred preferences be incorrect? (yes / no / unsure)
- Who may process your supplied task prompts? [study-specific multiple choice]

Incorrect answers trigger clarification and a second opportunity; final handling
must match the approved protocol.

## Per-response ratings

Show after each blinded response. Unless noted, use 1 = strongly disagree through
7 = strongly agree.

1. This is a good response for me for this task.
2. This response would help me understand the issue or take the next appropriate action.
3. This response is clear to me.
4. This response follows the response preferences relevant to this task.
5. This response stays relevant to what I asked.
6. This response feels appropriately personalized, if personalization is useful here.
7. The assistant appears to understand how I wanted this answer presented.

Technical depth (single choice): much too basic / slightly too basic / about
right / slightly too advanced / much too advanced.

Amount of detail (single choice): much too short / slightly too short / about
right / slightly too long / much too long.

Did you identify a material factual or safety problem? no / yes / unsure. If yes,
select a category and optionally explain without adding personal information.

## Pairwise response item

"Which response would you prefer to receive for this task?" (response A / no
preference / response B)

"What was the most important reason?" (more useful / better depth / clearer /
better amount of detail / better format / more accurate or safer / other with
optional short text)

The A/B position is randomized and is unrelated to experimental condition labels.

## Post-transparency block

Use 1 = strongly disagree through 7 = strongly agree:

- I could understand which preferences the system planned to use.
- I could understand why a displayed preference was present.
- I felt in control of the profile.
- It was easy to correct, lock, delete, or disable a preference.
- I would want a similar profile available across AI systems.
- I was comfortable with the types of preference evidence displayed.
- I worried that the profile might be used outside the context where it applies.

Behavioral record: selected item understood (yes/no); correct control located
(yes/no); requested change persisted in next preview (yes/no); completion time.

## Blinding and expectancy

Ask only after all primary response ratings:

- Did you think some answers were personalized? (yes / no / unsure)
- If yes, how confident were you? (1 not confident -- 7 extremely confident)
- What cues did you use? (optional, avoid condition names)

## Overall and open feedback

- How satisfied were you with the study interactions overall? (1--7)
- Which preference, if any, mattered most?
- Was any inferred preference incorrect, intrusive, or surprising?
- Is there a response preference you wanted but could not express?
- Any accessibility or usability problem? (Remind participant not to add private data.)

## Attention/quality item policy

Include at most two low-burden instructed-response items separated in the study.
Do not disguise them as preference questions. Quality exclusion requires the
preregistered rule plus corroborating evidence; no single opinion or open-text
answer is "incorrect."

## Coding

Keep original response categories. Do not treat ordinal ratings as equal-interval
without a documented sensitivity analysis. Bipolar depth/detail scores retain
direction; their absolute distance from "about right" is derived in analysis.
"No preference," "unsure," skipped, and inapplicable are distinct values.
